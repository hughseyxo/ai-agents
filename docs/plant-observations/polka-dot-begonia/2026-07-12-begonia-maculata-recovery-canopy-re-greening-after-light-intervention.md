# Begonia maculata recovery: canopy re-greening after light intervention

After roughly six weeks of documented, worsening light stress — red/coral/bronze pigmentation that had spread across ~90% of the canopy by mid-to-late June — the Begonia maculata is showing its first clear recovery. The central and lower leaves have re-greened to a deep, healthy base with crisp silver polka-dot spotting, the hallmark of the species when its light is correctly moderated. This is a meaningful milestone: it confirms the diagnosis held throughout was correct (a light/cultural problem, not overwatering or dehydration) and that the relocation away from the bright blinded window, sheer diffuser, and raised humidity — actions repeatedly flagged and finally carried out — have taken effect.

The recovery is partial, not complete. The far-left cascading stem still carries the old red/bronze, curled, dry-margined leaves, and the far-right outer leaf has a browned crisped tip. These are established stress-damaged leaves that will not re-green; they should be pruned so the plant channels energy into the new green growth rather than maintaining spent tissue. A single chlorotic yellow-green older leaf and minor brown fleck damage on the large lower-left leaf are cosmetic and consistent with the tail end of the stress episode.

Structurally the plant has never been in doubt — stems stayed green, thick and upright throughout, soil moist, turgor good. With the light problem now resolving, the care focus moves from 'rescue' to 'consolidate': hold the current diffused-light position (do not return it toward the window), keep humidity up to stop the remaining edge crisping, and clean up the dead material. A follow-up photo in about two weeks should confirm that newly unfurling leaves continue to emerge green, closing out what has been the plant's longest-running health issue on record.
