# Recovery confirmed: light stress resolved, green canopy restored

After a run of assessments escalating to **Concerning** (2026-06-16), where red/coral pigmentation had spread across 90%+ of the canopy with edge burn, this Begonia maculata has made a clear recovery. The mature leaves have regained a deep-green base with crisp, prominent silver-white spotting, and the vivid reddening has receded to only the newest upper growth and a few outer leaf edges — the normal cultivar colouration expected under healthy diffused light.

This outcome resolves the open question flagged repeatedly through May–June: whether the reddening was stress or cultivar colour. The answer is now evident — it was **light stress**. Once the plant was genuinely moved out of direct window light on 2026-06-19, with a sheer diffuser and raised humidity, new and mature foliage returned to green. This validates the earlier cultural diagnosis and the intervention taken.

The plant is not perfect: it has grown **leggy and sparse**, with tall bare canes and drooping leaves clustered at the ends, plus minor brown tip-crisping from low humidity. These are cosmetic/structural rather than health-threatening. Tip-pinching the upper canes will encourage a bushier habit, and continued humidity plus tip-trimming will tidy the edges.

Going forward, keep the plant in its current diffused-light position and maintain the existing 7-day watering schedule. The key lesson recorded here: this plant reds up under too much direct light and greens up when moved back — use foliage colour as an ongoing light-level gauge.
