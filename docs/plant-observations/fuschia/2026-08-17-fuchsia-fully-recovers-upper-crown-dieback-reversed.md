# Fuchsia fully recovers; upper-crown dieback reversed

The fuchsia has completed the recovery arc tracked since late July. Across the 07-30, 08-06 and 08-09 photos the plant was sparse and leggy with a recurring band of brown-crisped, mottled dieback on the upper crown near the fabric-bag opening — the driest zone of the vertical grow-bag, which dries top-first. In the current photo that crown has greened up entirely, pushing fresh foliage and new buds where dead tissue previously sat.

The rest of the plant matches: a dense, turgid, uniformly green canopy carrying numerous open magenta pendant blooms and a strong set of unopened buds through the mid and lower trailing stems. This is a clear step up in density from even the improved 08-09 photo, marking a genuine growth milestone rather than a simple hold.

The turnaround validates the standing guidance — canopy-rebuild tip-pinching plus deliberately slow top-watering so moisture reaches the upper crown instead of running off. The persistent crown crisping was correctly diagnosed as a bag-geometry/watering-method problem rather than a whole-plant water deficit, and correcting the watering method (not shortening the schedule) resolved it.

Going forward the plant needs only maintenance: routine deadheading of spent blooms, continued slow top-watering to protect the new crown growth, and occasional tip-pinching to hold the density. The 8-day watering cycle remains appropriate.
