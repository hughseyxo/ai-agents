# Fuchsia sudden decline — water stress at day 4

The vertical grow-bag fuchsia has taken a sharp turn after months of sustained good health. In the four days between the 2026-07-12 and 2026-07-16 photos, the plant went from a dense, uniformly green canopy with vigorous magenta flowering and a heavy bud set to a visibly stressed plant: the crown and upper section of the grow-bag are now largely bare and browned, and extensive brown, crisped leaf margins run down the mid and lower trailing stems. Remaining foliage looks limp and drooping rather than turgid, and the flower display has all but collapsed to a handful of blooms.

The pattern is telling. Damage is heaviest near the crown/top of the bag with greener survivors lower down — the mirror image of what you would expect from root-zone drying, and consistent with the uneven top-to-bottom drying of vertical grow-bags that has been flagged as a watch-point in every recent assessment. There is no sign of pest infestation or disease (no chewing, webbing, spotting, or sticky residue), so this reads as water/heat stress rather than a biological problem, likely compounded by warm outdoor conditions at only day 4 of the watering cycle.

The immediate action is to water thoroughly now rather than waiting for the scheduled day 7, soaking slowly so the whole column — especially the lower third — actually wets through. Strip out the browned leaves and spent blooms so the plant can redirect energy and so the next photo gives a clean read on recovery. Hold off on a formal frequency change for now: this is the first stress signal after a long healthy run, and one photo isn't enough to rebase the cadence. But check lower-bag moisture every day or two over the coming week; if the medium keeps drying out before day 7, that's the evidence needed to shorten the baseline frequency.
