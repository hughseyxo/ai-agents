# Fuschia crown regrows with new shoots post-crash

The fuschia has reached a clear recovery milestone following the mid-July water/heat crash. In the 07-16 crash photo the top third of the vertical grow-bag was a bare, browned dead crown mass; that dead tissue was cleared by 07-25, and as of 07-30 there are now vigorous fresh bright-green shoots pushing up **above** the top rim of the bag. This is genuine new leader growth from the crown zone, not just the mid-column foliage filling back in — a strong signal that the root zone has fully re-established and the plant is investing in new structure again.

Alongside the crown regrowth, flowering has begun to return. After the near-total bloom crash of mid-July (down to a single spent bloom), the 07-30 photo shows an open magenta bloom hanging at the base plus a fresh pink bud mid-column. The mid and lower canopy is now predominantly clean, turgid bright-green, with the crisped/mottled variegated crash damage reduced to a few lingering spent leaves on the trailing stems.

The recovery has been supported by the corrective watering on 07-16, a stable moisture regime since, and a half-strength high-potash feed applied 07-28. The next levers to consolidate this milestone are pinching the new top shoots once they have 2-3 leaf pairs (to drive branching into the still-sparse canopy), continuing the fortnightly potash feed, and removing the last brown-crisped leaves. No frequency change is warranted — this is the tail of a single crash-recovery arc, and the 7-day baseline remains appropriate.
