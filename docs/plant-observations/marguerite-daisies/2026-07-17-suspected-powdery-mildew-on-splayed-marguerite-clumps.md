# Suspected powdery mildew on splayed Marguerite clumps

A whitish, dusty coating has appeared on the foliage of the two splayed Marguerite clumps (front-left and lower-left) that lie flat against the soil surface. It was not present in the 2026-07-12 photo. The upright right/upper clumps, which sit above the soil in better airflow, appear clean. Some light brown leaf-tip flecking is also visible on the lower clump.

The distribution is the tell: powdery mildew (commonly *Podosphaera*/*Golovinomyces* spp. on Argyranthemum) thrives exactly here — dense, leggy growth lying on damp soil creates a still, humid microclimate. The alternative explanation is benign dried mineral/hard-water residue from overhead watering. To distinguish, wipe an affected leaf: mineral spotting rubs off cleanly and leaves healthy green tissue; mildew is a felty coating that returns and may sit on slightly distorted or yellowing leaf tissue.

If confirmed as mildew, act while it is localised: remove the worst-affected splayed growth (which also serves the long-overdue cut-back), improve airflow by lifting/pruning the sprawling clumps off the soil, avoid wetting the foliage when watering, and apply a potassium-bicarbonate or diluted-milk (1:9) spray to the remaining leaves. This dovetails with the structural pruning that has been outstanding since 2026-06-22 — one hard cut-back addresses both the legginess and the disease microclimate at once.

Re-photograph in ~1 week after the cut-back to confirm the coating has not spread to the healthy upright clumps and that compact regrowth is emerging clean.
