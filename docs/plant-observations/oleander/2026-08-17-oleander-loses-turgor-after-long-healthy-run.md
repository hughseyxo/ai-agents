# Oleander loses turgor after long healthy run

After a sustained healthy stretch from late July through 09 August — dense, deep-green, mounded canopy at full turgor with an ongoing pink bloom flush — this oleander shows its first clear stress signal on 2026-08-17. The whole canopy has lost turgor, with leaves drooping and folding downward, the colour has dulled from glossy deep green to a flatter grey-green, and the form has opened up and gone slightly leggy. Flowering has also stopped, with no open blooms or fresh bud trusses now visible.

Crucially, the plant was watered only two days ago (2026-08-15) on its effective 3-day schedule, yet it is wilting. That makes simple schedule lag unlikely as the sole cause. The most probable driver is heat/drought stress on a potted root ball sitting in a full-sun corner against a sun-baked fence and paving, where the pot can dry out well before the next scheduled watering. A less likely alternative is waterlogging/root stress, which can also present as wilt but with wet soil.

No yellowing, marginal scorch, leaf spotting, or visible aphids/scale were detected, so bacterial leaf scorch, nutrient deficiency, and pest infestation are not indicated at this stage. The immediate priority is a soil-moisture check: water deeply if dry, or hold and inspect drainage if the soil is still wet. Easing the plant's heat exposure and completing the overdue post-bloom deadhead and cut-back will support recovery.

This is logged as a watch item. If turgor rebounds after watering, it was transient heat/drought stress and the plant should return to its healthy baseline. If the wilt persists — especially with soil that stays wet — the next assessment should escalate to a full overwatering/root-rot investigation.
