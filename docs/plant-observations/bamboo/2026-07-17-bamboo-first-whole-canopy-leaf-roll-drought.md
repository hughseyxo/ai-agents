# Bamboo first whole-canopy leaf-roll (drought)

After a continuous run of Healthy assessments from 2026-06-16 through 2026-07-12, the potted Fargesia nigra showed its **first whole-canopy leaf-roll** on 2026-07-17 — only 5 days after its last watering and 2 days *before* the 7-day schedule would even flag it as due. Comparing the two photos directly: on 2026-07-12 the canopy was dense, deep green, and made of broad flat turgid blades; by 2026-07-17 those blades have furled into thin needle-like tubes across the whole canopy, the colour has dulled to a silvery grey-green, and the culms are splaying outward. This is the textbook true-thirst signal for container bamboo, which prior assessments had repeatedly named as the trigger to water immediately.

The cause is almost certainly the fast-drying container plus summer heat rather than disease or pests: the dark culms remain natural species pigmentation, and there is no chlorosis, scorch banding, fungal spotting or webbing. The browning has spread a little beyond the usual shaded lower-interior self-thinning into scattered mid-canopy tips, which is consistent with acute water stress rather than the slow inner-culin dieback tracked on earlier checks.

Action taken/advised: water deeply and immediately until it drains freely, then re-check in the evening — a purely thirsty bamboo should visibly un-roll and regreen within hours. Over the following week, check every 1-2 days and water on soil dryness or any return of leaf-roll rather than by the calendar. If the canopy does **not** recover after a good soak, escalate to checking root health and pot drainage.

This episode is the evidence base for shortening the summer watering cadence from 7 to ~5 days for this pot; revert toward 7 days as temperatures fall.
