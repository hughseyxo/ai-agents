# Lavender rapid dieback — possible collapse

Between the 2026-07-12 and 2026-07-15 photos (just 3 days) this outdoor potted lavender went from carrying substantial living silver-green foliage on both sides of the crown to being almost entirely tan-brown dead stalks and dried leaves, with only a few small surviving green tufts. This is a step-change beyond the slow, recurring post-bloom senescence that had been flagged since early June — that was always confined to the right-side spent flower stalks while the left/front crown stayed vigorous. Today the browning is whole-plant.

Because the soil surface is still dark and moist (watered 3 days ago) and there is no classic drought pattern of a wilting-but-living plant, this looks less like underwatering and more like an actual dieback. The most likely cause of a fast collapse in a container lavender is root or basal rot from overwatering/poor drainage, which is the single most common way potted lavender dies. It cannot be confirmed from the photo alone.

Recommended immediate steps: prune out all the dead brown growth back to living green wood (never into the bare woody base, which will not regenerate) so the true extent of survival becomes visible; inspect the base of the stems and the roots for grey/black discolouration, mushiness or a sour smell; and check that the pot and its saucer drain freely. Withhold watering until the soil has dried well down.

If, after clearing the dead material, only a handful of green tufts remain and the stem base is rotted, recovery is unlikely and replacement should be considered. If healthy green wood and firm roots are found, hard-prune to the living growth, improve drainage, and monitor for fresh regrowth over the next 2–3 weeks.
