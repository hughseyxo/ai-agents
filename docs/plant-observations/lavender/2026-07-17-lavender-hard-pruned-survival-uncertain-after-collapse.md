# Lavender hard-pruned; survival uncertain after collapse

The overdue post-bloom prune has now been done. Between the 2026-07-12 photo and today (2026-07-17), the dense upright crown of bare spent flower stalks and crisped tan-brown foliage has been cut away, leaving the pot roughly 80% bare dark soil scattered with loose cut stem and leaf debris. What remains alive is a small number of low silver-green tufts sprawling toward the front and right of the crown — a fraction of the foliage the plant carried a month ago.

This is the correct action for an overdue, woody, post-flowering lavender, but it has exposed how little living material is left after the sharp 2026-07-12→07-15 dieback. Recovery is not guaranteed: lavender only regenerates from green wood, never from bare woody base, so the handful of surviving green sprigs are the entire basis for any regrowth. They must be left completely undisturbed for the next few weeks.

The prime suspect for the rapid whole-plant collapse remains root/basal rot from overwatering — the most common killer of container lavender. The soil surface still reads dark and moist five days after the last watering, in full sun, which is a slow dry-down. The owner should NOT water, should confirm the pot and saucer drain freely, and should inspect the stem base and upper roots for grey/black discolouration, mushiness, or a sour smell to establish whether the plant is recoverable at all.

Immediate priorities: withhold water until the soil dries well down; clear the damp cut debris off the crown to reduce Botrytis grey-mould risk; inspect base/roots for rot; and then give the surviving green tufts 3-4 weeks in full sun to show whether they reshoot before deciding to keep or replace the plant.
