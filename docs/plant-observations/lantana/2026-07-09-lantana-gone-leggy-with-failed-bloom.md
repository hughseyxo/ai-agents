# Lantana gone leggy with failed bloom

The Lantana has regressed from the dense, compact, bushy form consistently recorded from early to mid-June into a **leggy, open habit**: long bare tan woody stems now radiate from a sparse, hollow interior, with leaves and buds concentrated only at the branch tips. Importantly, this is not a watering problem — leaf colour is a healthy deep green and turgor is strong, with no wilting or crispy edges. The soil is moist from watering 3 days ago.

The bloom has effectively **stalled and failed to open**. Across assessments the plant repeatedly formed bud clusters (open pale-yellow florets were briefly noted 2026-06-16) but by 2026-06-29 it had stalled at the unopened-bud stage, and this photo shows only small green immature bud spikes plus several spent/dried flower remnants — no open colourful clusters at all. Combined with heavy leaf growth, this points to the plant putting energy into foliage rather than flowers, which for Lantana usually means it needs a corrective prune and/or more direct sun and a potassium-rich feed.

Recommended intervention: cut the plant back by about a third, pruning the longest bare stems just above a leaf node to force lower branching and fresh flowering shoots; deadhead the spent spikes; and remove the yellowing/dropped leaves collecting in the pot. Follow with a light tomato-type feed and confirm genuine all-day full sun. This is the standard remedy when Lantana becomes leggy and bloom-shy, and should restore the compact, free-flowering form seen in June.
