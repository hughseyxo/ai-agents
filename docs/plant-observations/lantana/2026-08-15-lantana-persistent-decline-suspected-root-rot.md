# Lantana persistent decline — suspected root rot

This Lantana has now declined across four consecutive photo assessments (30 Jul → 09 Aug → 13 Aug → 15 Aug) with no recovery at any point, which elevates it from a routine watering-adjustment case to a plant in genuine trouble that warrants a standalone record.

The most significant new signal in the 15 Aug photo is a **change in symptom character**. Through 09–13 Aug the leaves were plump but limp and downturned on visibly moist soil, which read as overwatering. Now, three days after watering, the leaves are dull grey-green, curled inward and crispy, on a drier soil surface. A plant that stays wilted whether the soil is wet or dry, and keeps losing canopy despite a reduced watering cadence, is behaving like one whose **roots can no longer supply the top growth** — the classic aftermath of root rot caused by the earlier every-1-to-2-day watering.

Two confounders make an in-person check essential rather than another photo-only guess. First, the plant sits in a hot corner between a white rendered wall and dark paving in full sun — a reflective, high-heat microclimate that can desiccate a root-compromised plant quickly and mimic drought. Second, the whitefly/spider-mite inspection recommended on both 09 and 13 Aug has still not been carried out, and sap-sucking pests are the most common Lantana problem and a plausible driver of progressive thinning.

Recommended priority actions: unpot and inspect the roots for dark, soft, foul-smelling rot (repot into fresh free-draining mix if found); complete the pest inspection of leaf undersides and stems; hard-prune the leggy, bare and dead stems including the straggly right-hand branch to cut water demand and encourage bushy regrowth; and provide light afternoon shade during recovery. Water only by finger-testing the soil at 5cm depth rather than on a fixed calendar until the underlying cause is confirmed.
