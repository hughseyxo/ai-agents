# Lantana recovering after hard prune, new pink blooms

After four consecutive assessments of decline (30 Jul peak → progressively sparse, leggy and finally grey-green/crispy by 15 Aug), the Lantana has turned a corner. The hard prune recommended on 15 Aug has been carried out — the tall leggy central leader and the long straggly branch reaching toward the bench are both gone — and the plant has been moved back from the hot white-wall/bench corner to the shadier wooden-fence spot.

The visual signs of recovery are clear. Foliage is noticeably greener and more turgid, and the dull grey, inward-curled, desiccated leaves that dominated 13–15 Aug have largely resolved. Most notably, fresh pink/purple flower clusters have appeared — a new colour compared with the faint yellow/orange single cluster clinging on through the decline — which is a reliable indicator of returning vigour in Lantana.

The combination of improvement after (a) reducing the canopy load and (b) getting the plant out of the high-heat reflective microclimate reinforces the earlier hypothesis that the decline was driven by heat desiccation and/or root strain from over-frequent watering, rather than simple drought. Continuing to water by soil dryness rather than calendar is the right call while roots rebuild.

One caveat: the whitefly/spider-mite inspection recommended repeatedly since 09 Aug has still never been confirmed. Pests can quietly ride along under a recovering canopy, so this hands-on check should be completed now rather than deferred again.
