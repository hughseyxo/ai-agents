# New growth flush after hydration recovery

The outdoor Lantana has moved from acute wilt (2026-07-15) through hydration recovery (2026-07-16 watering, confirmed 07-17) and is now showing a distinct flush of **new vegetative growth**. In the 2026-07-20 photo, fresh green shoots are emerging along the mid and lower sections of the previously bare tan woody stems, and new growth is pushing from nodes near the base of the plant — not just clustered at the tips as in every prior assessment. Foliage is upright, turgid and glossy deep green, and the plant is now cane-staked.

This is a meaningful milestone: for weeks the framework was criticised as a sparse, hollow, leggy structure with foliage only at the tips. The new basal and mid-stem shoots are exactly what a tip prune is meant to encourage, and they suggest the plant has the vigour to bush out and rebuild a fuller form. The 3-day summer watering cadence is clearly supporting this — soil is dark and moist and there is no sign of the dry-out stress that caused the mid-July wilt.

The one unresolved issue is flowering. Despite the vegetative rebound, bloom is still stalled at small green immature bud spikes with no open florets — consistent across all four trend points. This continues to point at a nutrient/light bias toward leaves over flowers rather than a health problem.

Actionable follow-up: capitalise on the new growth with a light tip prune of the longest bare stems (just above nodes bearing new shoots) to force compact branching, deadhead the stalled bud spikes, and apply a potassium-rich (tomato) feed while confirming true all-day full sun to finally push the plant into bloom.
