# Canopy thinning and leaf drop after July peak

The Lantana has visibly declined between its 2026-07-30 peak and 2026-08-09. On 30 Jul the plant was dense, compact and carrying green flower-bud clusters — the culmination of a strong recovery through late July. The current photo shows a noticeably thinner canopy: more bare woody interior stems are exposed, remaining foliage is concentrated toward the outer tips, several leaves look limp and downturned, and the bud clusters seen previously are no longer visible.

The most likely driver is watering cadence. The plant thrived in June and July on roughly a 5-day interval, but the current schedule is every 1 day. Lantana is drought-tolerant and resents constantly wet roots. Leaf drop and canopy thinning occurring while the soil surface remains dark and moist is the classic signature of over-frequent watering rather than underwatering, and left unchecked it can progress to root rot.

A pest cause should be ruled out in parallel. Sudden canopy thinning is also a hallmark of whitefly (the most common Lantana pest) or spider mites. The owner should shake the canopy to look for clouds of tiny white insects and check leaf undersides and sparse stems for fine webbing.

Recommended response: stop daily watering, test soil moisture at depth and only water when partly dry (targeting ~4-day intervals adjusted for weather), verify free drainage, inspect closely for pests, and lightly tip-prune leggy bare stems to encourage the compact bushy regrowth seen at the plant's peak. This warrants follow-up at the next photo to confirm whether reducing watering restores turgor and density.
