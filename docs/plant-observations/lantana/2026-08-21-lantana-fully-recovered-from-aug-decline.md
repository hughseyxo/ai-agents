# Lantana fully recovered from Aug decline

The Lantana has completed a decisive recovery from the July-August decline that bottomed out on 15 Aug, when the plant was at its most deteriorated — sparse canopy, extensive bare woody stems, and dull grey-green crispy inward-curled leaves. Today's photo shows a full, bright-green, turgid canopy with an obvious upright fresh-growth flush clothing the previously bare interior stems. This is the strongest vegetative state since the 30 Jul peak.

The turnaround tracks three interventions applied after the 15 Aug low point: relocating the pot out of the hot reflective white-wall/bench corner into the shadier wooden-fence corner, a hard prune of the leggy central leader and straggly right-hand branch, and a switch from calendar watering to watering by soil dryness. Fresh green clippings on the paving in this frame confirm continued deadheading/tidy pruning. The 17 Aug assessment caught the first reversal (greener foliage plus new pink flower clusters); this observation confirms it has held and built further.

The recovery pattern reinforces the working diagnosis: the decline was driven by heat desiccation plus root strain from over-frequent watering, not simple drought. That is why reducing heat exposure, cutting canopy demand, and easing off the watering cadence worked. The practical implication is to keep watering by finger-test at depth rather than reverting to a fixed schedule, and to keep the plant in the cooler fence position.

One caveat: the whitefly/spider-mite inspection recommended across four prior assessments has still never been confirmed. A denser recovering canopy is exactly where an early sap-sucking infestation can hide, so the pest check should be closed out now rather than deferred again.
