# Root inspection: white/dry roots — hydrophobic soil, not rot

## Finding
On unpotting 2026-07-29, roots were **white and dry** — no soft brown rot, no sour smell. Root rot diagnosis ruled out.

## Revised Diagnosis: Hydrophobic Soil / Moisture Delivery Failure
The old substrate had likely dried out enough to become water-repellent (a common issue with peat-heavy mixes). Water was running down the sides of the pot and out the drainage hole without penetrating to the root zone — hence: moist-looking surface, dry roots, wilting despite "recent" watering. The necrosis and dieback were from prolonged covert root-zone drought, not rot.

## Implication for Recovery
Prognosis significantly improved. Healthy white roots in fresh, evenly-moist soil should resume water uptake normally. Expect visible recovery (turgor returning to remaining stems) within a few days rather than weeks.

## Revised Care Plan
- **Water now or very soon** — the roots were dry; the fresh soil needs to be evenly moistened. Water slowly and thoroughly until it drains from the bottom.
- Resume the 4-day baseline schedule, but **check soil at 3-4cm depth** before each watering going forward (the old hydrophobic issue masked true dryness).
- Continue removing dead/shrivelled leaves and brown tendrils.
- Afternoon shade still advisable while the remaining leaf mass is reduced — fewer leaves = less transpiration buffer.
- Do NOT add fertiliser until clear new growth is visible.
