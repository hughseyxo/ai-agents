# Scratch test confirms live green base

After the scratch test had been outstanding for three consecutive assessments, today's close-up finally shows it done: the owner is holding a basal stem whose scratched lower end, near the soil line, reads a clear pale green-yellow. **This is live cambium — the plant's base/crown is still alive**, even though virtually all of the above-ground framework is now confirmed dead.

This is a genuine turning point. Across Aug 10–17 the top-growth deteriorated to a uniformly straw-tan, brittle skeleton with shrivelled tendrils and no surviving green in the canopy, and the read had shifted to 'probable full failure'. The green at the base changes that: frost-hardy passiflora routinely lose all top-growth yet regenerate from live crown/root tissue, so there is a real recovery path here rather than a dead plant.

The action that follows is now unambiguous. Cut the confirmed-dead brown stems back to just above where the green tissue begins near the base, scratch-testing each stem as you go so you only remove genuinely dead sections. Leave the green basal stub and crown completely undisturbed — that is the sole recovery tissue.

Care through recovery: keep the soil only lightly moist (finger-check daily; the 1-day effective cadence is far too wet for a leafless plant and risks rotting the live roots), revert to the 4-day baseline managed by moisture, and shade the exposed base during the hottest hours so the surviving cambium isn't cooked against the brick wall. Re-photograph the base in about a week to watch for new shoots emerging from the crown.
