# Sudden whole-plant wilt and canopy collapse

The passionflower has taken a sharp, unexpected turn. After a solid 38-day run of excellent vegetative health — dense, turgid, dark-green deeply-lobed foliage, active tendrils, and even the first fruit/ovaries forming since mid-July — the newest photo (2026-07-25) shows the whole plant wilting. Leaves are drooping and hanging limp right across the canopy, the foliage has thinned dramatically with bare trellis now visible, and the colour has dulled to a greyish-green with some browning/drying leaves.

What makes this concerning rather than routine is the timing: the plant was watered only 2 days ago on a 4-day cadence, so this is not a simple case of the pot drying out on schedule. A sudden, whole-plant collapse of a previously vigorous vine has three plausible causes worth checking in order: (1) acute heat/transpiration stress — a potted plant in full sun during a hot spell can lose water faster than the roots can supply it, even in moist soil; (2) root rot — if the substrate is actually soggy, the roots may be failing and unable to move water up; or (3) a stem or root problem such as mechanical damage, girdling, or a wilt disease (Fusarium/Passiflora wilt), which can cause rapid one-sided or whole-plant collapse.

Immediate diagnostics: feel the soil 3-4cm down. Dry means water deeply now and shade the pot. Soggy means hold water, improve drainage, and inspect the roots. Either way, check the main stem at the pot base and the lower vines for splits, soft brown rot, or girdling, since those would explain a collapse that watering won't fix.

This interrupts both the healthy vegetative streak and the slowly-emerging flowering/fruiting response tracked since 2026-07-15. It should be re-photographed and reassessed within a couple of days to see whether the plant rehydrates and firms back up, or continues to decline — the latter would point firmly at a root or vascular problem rather than transient heat stress.
