# Passionflower root-rot progression to severe dieback

The passionflower has crossed from stress into serious, progressive decline. Across five photos the vine went from a full healthy canopy to an acute wilt on 2026-07-25, a genuine partial recovery on 07-27, a relapse with early leaf necrosis on 07-28, and now (07-29) pronounced dieback: the trellis is largely bare, remaining leaves are shrivelled, curled and grey-green, and there are numerous dried brown tendrils and a yellowing leaf. This is the worst frame of the sequence.

The decisive diagnostic signal is that the plant keeps wilting — and is now dying back — while the pot substrate still looks dark and moist four days after watering. Heat/transpiration stress rehydrates within a day or two of watering (as the 07-27 recovery showed); a plant that wilts and necrotises on moist soil is almost certainly suffering root rot or otherwise impaired water uptake. Adding water in this state makes it worse.

Recommended response is to stop treating this as a hydration problem and go to the roots. Unpot the plant, inspect the root ball for soft, brown, mushy roots and a sour/rotten smell (versus firm, pale, healthy roots), cut away all rotten material, and repot into fresh free-draining compost in a clean pot. Strip off all shrivelled and necrotic foliage down to live green stem, move the pot into afternoon shade, and withhold water until the top few centimetres are confirmed dry.

Watering frequency should not be changed — the 4-day cadence is not the cause. The main stems and gripping tendrils are still green, so the plant is not necessarily lost, but recovery now depends on resolving the root/drainage problem rather than on any watering adjustment. If the roots are extensively rotten, prognosis is guarded.
