# Passionflower top-growth appears fully died back

As of 2026-08-13 the passionflower's entire above-ground framework has gone straw-tan and brittle with no visible green tissue remaining. This marks the completion of a steady decline tracked since Aug 9, when the main climbing stems were still broadly green and turgid. The green receded to short upper segments by Aug 11 and has now disappeared entirely, with the old tendrils shrivelled and the stems hanging loose off the trellis.

Crucially, there have still been no reshoots or bud-break anywhere on the plant, roughly ten days after the Aug 3 strip-back and well beyond the expected one-week window for a healthy passionflower to push new growth. No signs of pests (no mite stippling, webbing, or aphids) — this is desiccation/dieback of the above-ground stems.

The outstanding question is whether the crown and roots are still alive. A scratch test on the central base stem and an upper stem segment is the decisive next step and has now been recommended for three consecutive assessments without a reported result. If cambium anywhere scratches moist green, that tissue must be preserved and the plant given more time. If everything scratches dry brown, the visible top-growth has failed and should be cut back to a short basal framework.

Even in the worst case, this is not necessarily the end: frost-hardy passiflora frequently regenerate from the roots after all top-growth dies. Reverting to less frequent, moisture-managed watering (the 4-day baseline rather than the current daily cadence) is important now to avoid rotting any surviving roots while they attempt to push fresh shoots from the crown.
