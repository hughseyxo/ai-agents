# Passionflower drought collapse — near-total defoliation, stems still green

The passionflower has suffered a dramatic drought/heat collapse between 30 July and 2 August 2026. In June it was a vigorous climber with dense, deeply-lobed dark-green foliage covering the full height of the trellis. Over four photos it has lost almost all of that foliage: 30 July shows heavy wilting, marginal browning and leaf drop; 31 July shows progressive defoliation with limp, curling leaves and straw-brown tendrils; and today (2 August) only a handful of shrivelled brown leaves remain on otherwise bare stems.

The most important observation is that the main climbing stems are **still green and firm**, not shrivelled or brown. Passionflowers are extremely resilient and routinely reshoot from a green framework after hard setbacks, so this is very likely a recoverable acute stress event rather than the death of the plant. The trigger appears to be drought/heat: last watered 29 July (four days before this photo) against a full-sun brick wall during an August heatwave, with the pot's soil surface looking dry.

Recovery plan: give an immediate deep soak (and re-check that evening, since bone-dry compost can shed water), then hold off on any hard pruning for 2-3 weeks — remove only the obviously dead brown leaves and crispy tips, leaving every green stem in place to see where it reshoots. Provide temporary afternoon shade while the plant is leafless, and check surviving growth for spider mites, which flourish in these hot dry conditions.

Process note: this collapse happened within the nominal watering window, which points to the summer cadence being too slack for this exposed, full-sun position. Recommend tightening the watering baseline through the hot months and scheduling a follow-up photo in ~2 weeks to confirm regrowth.
