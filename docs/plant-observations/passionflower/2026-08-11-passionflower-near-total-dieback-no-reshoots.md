# Passionflower near-total dieback, no reshoots

The passionflower has reached a critical decline milestone. Following the Aug 3 strip-back, the plant was expected to break bud and reshoot from surviving green stems within roughly a week. Across the Aug 6 → Aug 9 → Aug 10 → Aug 11 photo sequence, the surviving green tissue has instead steadily receded and, in today's image, is essentially gone — nearly the entire trellis framework now reads straw-tan and crispy, with at most a faint trace of green near the upper clips. No reshoots have appeared anywhere, well past the anticipated bud-break window.

This is desiccation/dieback rather than a pest or disease event: there is no spider-mite stippling or webbing and no aphid activity on the remaining stems. The likely drivers are the severe post-strip-back stress compounded by a full-sun position against a heat-radiating brick wall on a leafless plant with no canopy to transpire and cool itself.

The decisive next step is a scratch test on the central vertical stem and the upper stem segments by the green clips. Moist green cambium beneath the bark means the plant is still alive and worth continued moisture-managed care with midday shade; dry brown cambium all the way through confirms those sections are dead. Any confirmed-dead stems can be cut back — potentially to the base — after which the crown and roots should be watched for basal regrowth, since frost-hardy passifloras can resprout from the roots even after the top growth is lost.

Watering should be held on the tight 2-day cadence: a leafless plant uses almost no water, and the Aug 9 watering should still have the root ball moist. Reverting to the 4-day baseline and managing strictly by soil moisture avoids waterlogging the roots during this fragile phase.
