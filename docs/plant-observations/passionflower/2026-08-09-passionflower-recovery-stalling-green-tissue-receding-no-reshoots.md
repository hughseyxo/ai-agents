# Passionflower recovery stalling — green tissue receding, no reshoots

One week on from the drought strip-back to a bare framework, the passionflower has not yet produced any reshoots, and the surviving green tissue is now visibly shrinking rather than holding steady. Across the Aug 4 → Aug 5 → Aug 6 → Aug 9 photo sequence, the broadly green, turgid climbing stems of a few days ago have given way to a framework in which green survives mainly on the central vertical stem and the upper segments held by the green trellis clips; most of the thinner mid and lower stems and old tendrils have dried to straw-tan.

This matters because the earlier assessments were built on a "critical positive": the main framework was green and turgid, marking the plant as a live, recoverable specimen. That positive is now partially eroding. Passionflower reshoots vigorously from green stems, so as long as a band of live cambium remains — most reliably on the central stem and near the clips — recovery is still plausible. But the receding green combined with the absence of bud break at the point where reshoots were expected shifts this from "paused recovery" toward "stalling."

The care emphasis changes accordingly. The plant has now gone 4 days since its last water (Aug 5), so the earlier waterlogging risk from over-frequent watering has passed; the priority flips to keeping the live roots lightly moist so they can power new growth. Continue holding the framework unpruned — cut nothing until a stem is brown and crisp all the way through — and keep midday shade on the bare stems.

Set a firm checkpoint around Aug 14-16: if there are still no reshoots and green tissue keeps retreating, gently scratch a main stem with a thumbnail. Bright green underneath means keep waiting; brown and dry means that section is dead and viability should be reassessed. This is the observation that flips the plant from "recovering" to genuinely at-risk if the trend continues.
