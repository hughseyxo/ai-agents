# Passionflower recovers — 07-25 collapse was heat stress

The passionflower has fully recovered from the sharp collapse recorded on 2026-07-25. In today's photo (2026-07-27) the entire canopy is turgid and upright again — leaves held firm and deep glossy green, tendrils curling and gripping the trellis, and the vine climbing vertically. This is a decisive reversal of the limp, drooping, dull-grey wilt that appeared only two days into the 4-day watering cycle five days ago.

The recovery resolves the open diagnostic question from that incident. A previously vigorous vine that wilts abruptly could be suffering heat/transpiration stress, root rot, or a stem/wilt disease. Because this plant rehydrated within roughly two days of watering, with no lasting leaf loss, stem damage or continued decline, the cause is confirmed as **transient heat/transpiration stress** on a full-sun potted plant — root rot or a vascular wilt disease would not rebound this cleanly or quickly.

The practical takeaway is that the 4-day baseline cadence is adequate and does not need changing; the collapse was a one-off hot-day event, not chronic under-watering. The standing risk is repeat transpiration stress on the hottest days, since a potted root ball in full sun overheats and can't keep pace with water demand. Pre-empting that with light afternoon shade (or shading the pot) during heatwaves is more effective than reacting after the leaves have already collapsed.

Only minor cosmetic cleanup remains — a few dried brown tendrils and spent lower leaves from the wilt — and the trailing leaders can be re-tied onto the upper trellis to restore vertical coverage and encourage the stalled flowering/fruiting response now that vegetative health is back to the 07-15/07-20 baseline.
