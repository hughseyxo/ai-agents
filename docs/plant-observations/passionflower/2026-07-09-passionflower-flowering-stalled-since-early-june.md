# Passionflower flowering stalled since early June

Despite a month of vigorous vegetative growth in full sun, this passionflower has produced no flowers since green buds were last recorded on 2026-06-04. Today's photo (2026-07-09) shows dense, healthy dark-green foliage and active tendrils but not a single bud or bloom anywhere on the plant. This confirms the stalled-flowering pattern the intelligence runs have flagged repeatedly (2026-07-05, 07-07, 07-08).

The most likely cause is vegetative vigour driven by excess nitrogen: passiflora will pour energy into leaf and tendril growth at the expense of flowering when soil nitrogen is high, which fits the very lush, leaf-heavy appearance here. Other contributors can be a pot that is not yet root-restricted (passionflowers often flower better slightly pot-bound) and heavy soft growth shading the older wood where flowers set.

Recommended intervention: stop any high-nitrogen feeding and switch to a high-potash fertiliser (e.g. tomato feed) every 1–2 weeks through the summer to push flower initiation. Retrain the loose leaders up onto the now-bare upper trellis so more growth sits in direct sun, and avoid hard pruning during the flowering window since blooms form on new season's growth.

Structurally, note also that the canopy has slumped into the lower two-thirds of the support and pulled away from the upper grid — a change from the full-height coverage seen in the June assessments. Re-tying the vine will both improve the look and expose more growth to the light it needs to bloom. Recheck for buds in ~2 weeks after the feed change.
