# Passionflower re-lapse — suspected root problem

The passionflower has relapsed after the recovery recorded on 2026-07-27, and the picture has shifted from a transient-heat-stress story to a possible root problem. In today's photo (2026-07-28) the canopy is again drooping and limp with a dull grey-green cast, well short of the firm, glossy, densely-leaved state seen on 2026-07-20. The recovery flagged three days ago now looks to have been overstated — the plant never fully returned to baseline and has slipped back.

The key change is the combination of two signals: (1) new brown necrotic patches and dried margins on several upper-mid leaves, i.e. actual tissue dieback rather than reversible loss of turgor; and (2) the pot substrate appearing dark and moist despite the last watering being three days ago. Wilting on a moist pot is the classic signature of impaired water uptake — typically root rot or root damage — rather than thirst. The earlier working theory (transient heat/transpiration stress) was supported by the fast bounce-back after 07-25, but that bounce-back has not held.

Recommended follow-through over the next few days: probe the soil 3-4cm down and inspect the main stem base and surface roots for soft brown rot, sogginess or a sour smell. If the roots/soil are wet, withhold watering (do not water on the 07-29 cadence date), improve drainage, and let the medium dry out before watering again. Remove the necrotic leaves and spent tendrils. If wilt continues on drying soil, consider unpotting to check the rootball directly. The main stems are still green and upright with tendrils gripping, so the plant is far from lost — but this needs close monitoring, and the watering schedule should not be increased, as more water would worsen a root-rot scenario.
