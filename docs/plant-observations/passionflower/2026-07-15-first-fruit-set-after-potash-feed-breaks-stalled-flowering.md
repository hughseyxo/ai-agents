# First fruit set after potash feed breaks stalled flowering

After a 38+ day stalled-flowering stretch (buds last seen 2026-06-04) driven by vegetative vigour / likely excess nitrogen, the passionflower is showing its first reproductive activity in the 2026-07-15 photo: several small green developing fruit/ovaries are visible mid-frame that were absent in the 2026-07-12 assessment. This lines up temporally with the high-potash (tomato) feed and stem retraining completed 2026-07-12, which were prescribed specifically to push the plant out of leaf-only growth.

The response window is consistent with expectations — potassium promotes flower/fruit initiation, and a shift within ~3 days of the first feed suggests buds may already have been forming and the feed helped them set rather than abort. This is an encouraging early signal, not yet a confirmed sustained bloom cycle.

Alongside the fruit set, trellis coverage has climbed higher than in the 2026-07-12 photo, partially reversing the lower-two-thirds regression noted on 2026-07-09 and 2026-07-12. The retraining onto the grid is taking hold and exposing more growth to full sun, reinforcing the flowering push.

Recommendation: hold the course — continue the weekly high-potash feed, keep withholding high-nitrogen feeds, and keep tying leaders up onto the bare upper grid. Watch over the next 1–2 weeks to confirm the fruit develop and, ideally, that open blooms follow. If the ovaries abort, revisit whether light or pot-binding is limiting the flowering rather than nutrition alone.
