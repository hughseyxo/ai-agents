### 2026-08-09 — Healthy
Fifth+ photo assessment and the first clear recovery milestone. Comparing the batch chronologically (Jul 12 → Jul 20 → Jul 30 → Aug 9), the plant has decisively reversed the downward trend that peaked at Concerning on 2026-06-16. The mature canopy is now predominantly deep green with crisp, bright silver-white polka-dot spotting; the large lower and mid leaves in particular hold a strong green base. The near-universal red/coral/bronze pigmentation that dominated in June has receded to just the thin upper new-growth cane and a few outer leaf edges/undersides — this is normal Begonia maculata cultivar colouration under adequate diffused light, NOT the sustained light stress seen previously. This confirms the 2026-06-19 intervention (moved away from direct window, sheer diffuser added, humidity raised) was effective and that the earlier reddening was indeed light/cultural stress that has now resolved. Core structure remains sound: stems green, firm, upright, no rot or pests. Two residual, minor issues remain: (1) the habit is leggy and sparse — tall bare canes with leaves bunched at the ends and several lower leaves drooping/cascading, an open stretched silhouette that would benefit from tip-pinching to promote branching; and (2) a few leaf tips show minor brown crisping consistent with low humidity, not dehydration. Soil surface is dark and moist (watered 2026-08-08, 1 day ago); do NOT water early — 7-day schedule remains appropriate and turgor is fine. Recommend pinching the leggy upper canes, trimming crispy tips, maintaining humidity, keeping the current diffused-light spot, and optionally staking the floppy canes.

**Recommended next steps:**
- (medium) Pinch/prune the leggy upper cane tips (the tall thin stems above the main leaf cluster) by a node or two to encourage branching and a bushier, more compact habit — The plant has become tall, bare-stemmed and open; tip-pruning redirects energy into lateral shoots and fills out the silhouette
- (medium) Snip off the few brown crispy leaf tips and keep humidity raised (pebble tray or group with other plants) — Removes dead tissue and addresses the residual edge-crisping, which is a low-humidity symptom, not a watering one
- (low) Keep the plant in its current diffused-light position — do not move it back toward the bright window — The return of green foliage confirms the reduced-light location resolved the June light stress; reverting would risk re-reddening
- (low) Add a small stake or support for the drooping/cascading canes if a tidier shape is wanted — The long canes flop under leaf weight; light support keeps foliage presentable while lateral growth develops
