# Plant Species Reference

Species-specific visual health indicators, stress signs, common issues, pruning guidance, and seasonal notes. Used by the photo assessment system to anchor model output to known species behaviour.

---

## Monstera Deliciosa

**Healthy indicators:** Large, glossy dark-green leaves with firm texture; mature leaves develop fenestrations (splits/holes); new growth unfurls as pale green; aerial roots present but not excessive.

**Underwatering:** Leaves droop and lose turgor, edges may curl inward; soil visibly dry and pulling from pot edges; new growth stunted or slow.

**Overwatering:** Yellow lower leaves; soft, mushy stems at soil level; dark or waterlogged soil; possible root rot smell; yellowing spreads upward from oldest leaves.

**Light stress:** Pale or washed-out leaves = too much direct sun; very dark green with small, non-fenestrated leaves = insufficient light.

**Common issues:** Scale insects (brown bumps on stems/leaf undersides); spider mites (fine webbing, stippled leaves); root rot from poor drainage.

**Pruning:** Remove dead or yellow leaves at the base. Trim aerial roots only if they're obstructing — do not remove all of them. Shape by cutting stems just above a node. Spring/early summer is optimal.

**Seasonal notes:** Growth slows significantly in winter; reduce watering and do not fertilise.

## Polka Dot Plant

**Healthy indicators:** Vivid pink/red/white spotted leaves with bright colour contrast against green; compact, bushy growth; leaves slightly firm.

**Underwatering:** Leaves wilt and droop rapidly; colour fades; edges brown and crispy.

**Overwatering:** Yellow leaves, especially lower ones; stems become soft or leggy; soggy soil.

**Light stress:** Colour fades to pale in low light; bleaches out in direct harsh sun. Needs bright indirect light for best colour.

**Common issues:** Leggy growth (insufficient light); powdery mildew in humid stagnant conditions.

**Pruning:** Pinch growing tips regularly (every 2–3 weeks) to maintain bushy shape — otherwise becomes leggy quickly. Cut stems back by a third if already leggy. Remove any flower spikes promptly as flowering signals the plant's end-of-life.

**Seasonal notes:** Short-lived plant; may need replacing annually. Blooming = senescence approaching.

## Aloe Vera

**Healthy indicators:** Thick, plump, upright grey-green leaves with slightly serrated edges; firm to the touch with gel visible when a leaf is cut; compact rosette form.

**Underwatering:** Leaves thin, wrinkled, or concave; tips may brown and dry; rosette collapses inward.

**Overwatering:** Leaves turn soft, translucent, or mushy; yellowing at base; brown soft rot at soil level; plant leans or collapses.

**Light stress:** Red or orange tinting = too much direct sun or drought stress; etiolated (tall, pale, reaching) = insufficient light.

**Common issues:** Root rot (most common cause of death); mealy bugs at leaf bases; scale on stems.

**Pruning:** Remove dead or dried outer leaves by cutting cleanly at the base. Do not cut healthy leaves unless harvesting gel. Remove offsets (pups) when they are a third of the parent's size — repot separately.

**Seasonal notes:** Near-dormant in winter; water very sparingly (monthly). Outdoors in summer if frost-free.

## Dracaena Trifasciata (Snake Plant)

**Healthy indicators:** Upright, firm, sword-like leaves with clear yellow margins (on variegated varieties); deep green banding; slow but consistent upright growth.

**Underwatering:** Leaves wrinkle slightly or lose rigidity; tips brown and dry; very slow to show — this plant is extremely drought-tolerant.

**Overwatering:** Soft, mushy base; yellowing leaves that collapse at soil level; brown mushy rot starting from roots. This is the most common cause of decline.

**Light stress:** Tolerates low light but grows slowly; very bright direct sun can scorch leaf edges brown.

**Common issues:** Root rot from overwatering (most common); spider mites in dry conditions; mealy bugs.

**Pruning:** Cut individual leaves at the base if damaged, brown-tipped, or dead. Trim brown tips with scissors following the leaf's natural point shape. Divide by cutting rhizome when pot-bound.

**Seasonal notes:** Very slow grower in winter; water once a month at most. Extremely drought-hardy.

## Yucca Plant

**Healthy indicators:** Stiff, sword-like green leaves radiating from a woody trunk or cane; firm texture; lower leaves naturally yellow and drop over time as the trunk forms.

**Underwatering:** Leaves lose rigidity and droop; brown dry tips spread inward; soil bone dry.

**Overwatering:** Yellow soft leaves; brown mushy rot at the trunk base; soggy soil; root rot.

**Light stress:** Needs full sun or very bright indirect light; pale, floppy growth in low light.

**Common issues:** Scale insects on trunk and stems; root rot; brown tips from fluoride in tap water.

**Pruning:** Remove dead lower leaves by pulling down firmly or cutting at the stem. Trim brown tips following the leaf shape. Cut the main trunk to encourage branching — new growth emerges below the cut. Best done in spring.

**Seasonal notes:** Tolerates dry indoor winter conditions well; reduce watering significantly in winter.

## Oleander

**Healthy indicators:** Lance-shaped deep green leaves; abundant flowers (pink/red/white/yellow); woody stems; vigorous growth in warm weather.

**Underwatering:** Leaf wilt and drop; flowers drop prematurely; older leaves yellow and fall first.

**Overwatering:** Root rot; yellowing leaves; waterlogged soil; poor flowering.

**Light stress:** Needs full sun for flowering; shaded plants grow leggy with few or no flowers.

**Common issues:** Aphids (clusters on new growth); scale; oleander leaf scorch (bacterial). Note: all parts are highly toxic.

**Pruning:** Prune after main flowering flush — cut stems back by up to a third to encourage new flowering growth. Deadhead spent blooms. Shape in late summer/autumn. Wear gloves — sap is toxic.

**Seasonal notes:** Semi-dormant in cold winters; reduce watering. Flowers on new growth, so annual pruning improves flowering.

## Lavender

**Healthy indicators:** Grey-green aromatic foliage; upright woody stems; abundant purple/blue flower spikes in bloom; compact mounded form.

**Underwatering:** Wilting foliage; grey-green turns silvery-pale; flower spikes droop.

**Overwatering:** Grey or black stem rot at the base; yellowing foliage; mushy woody stems; most common cause of death in containers.

**Light stress:** Needs full sun minimum 6 hours; insufficient light = leggy growth, poor flowering, increased disease susceptibility.

**Common issues:** Root rot (overwatering); grey mould (Botrytis) in damp conditions; shab (woody dieback). Needs excellent drainage.

**Pruning:** Cut back by one-third to one-half after flowering — do not cut into old woody growth with no green leaves, it will not regenerate. Trim in early spring to shape. Annual pruning essential to prevent woodiness and extend plant life.

**Seasonal notes:** Mostly dormant in winter; almost no watering needed. Prune in late summer after bloom and again lightly in spring.

## Passionflower

**Healthy indicators:** Vigorous climbing stems with tendrils; deeply lobed green leaves; exotic multi-part flowers (if in bloom); fast-growing in warm conditions.

**Underwatering:** Wilting tendrils and leaves; leaf drop; slowed growth.

**Overwatering:** Yellow leaves; root rot; sluggish growth; soggy soil.

**Light stress:** Needs full sun to part shade for flowering; too much shade = leaves only, no flowers.

**Common issues:** Spider mites in hot dry conditions (look for stippled leaves, webbing); aphids on new growth; caterpillar damage (chewed leaves).

**Pruning:** Cut back hard in early spring — can reduce to a framework of main stems. Deadhead spent flowers. Remove dead or damaged stems throughout the season. Tolerates very hard pruning and responds with vigorous regrowth.

**Seasonal notes:** Dies back to ground level in hard frosts (frost-hardy species will regrow from roots in spring). Cut back in autumn after flowering; clear dead growth in spring.

## Lantana

**Healthy indicators:** Dense clusters of small multicoloured flowers (orange/yellow/red/pink); rough-textured aromatic leaves; bushy compact growth.

**Underwatering:** Wilting; flower and leaf drop; dry crispy leaf edges.

**Overwatering:** Yellow leaves; root rot; poor flowering; waterlogged soil.

**Light stress:** Needs full sun for prolific flowering; shaded plants produce foliage but few flowers.

**Common issues:** Whitefly (common — look for clouds of tiny white insects when disturbed); spider mites; powdery mildew in humid shade.

**Pruning:** Deadhead spent flower clusters to encourage continuous bloom. Cut back by a third in spring to promote bushy growth. Can be cut back hard if leggy. Remove any frost-damaged growth in spring.

**Seasonal notes:** Tender perennial — frost-sensitive. Cut back in autumn in cold climates; overwinter indoors or treat as annual.

## Fuschia

**Healthy indicators:** Pendulous bi-coloured flowers (purple/red/pink/white); mid-green oval leaves with slight sheen; compact trailing or bushy habit depending on variety.

**Underwatering:** Wilting (often sudden); bud and flower drop; leaf drop; very sensitive to drying out.

**Overwatering:** Yellow leaves; root rot; soggy soil; poor flowering.

**Light stress:** Prefers bright indirect light or morning sun; afternoon full sun causes wilting and scorching. Hates hot dry wind.

**Common issues:** Vine weevil (larvae eat roots — plant suddenly collapses); whitefly; fuchsia gall mite (causes distorted stunted growth at shoot tips).

**Pruning:** Deadhead consistently for continuous flowering. Pinch growing tips to encourage bushiness. Cut back hard in spring (reduce all stems to 2–3 buds). Remove gall-mite-affected shoot tips immediately and dispose — do not compost.

**Seasonal notes:** Dies back in frost; overwinter in cool frost-free location with minimal watering. New growth in spring — cut back to live wood after last frost.

## Marguerite Daisies

**Healthy indicators:** Bright white/yellow/pink daisy flowers with yellow centres; fine feathery aromatic foliage; compact bushy mound; prolific flowering from spring to autumn.

**Underwatering:** Wilting; flowers and buds drop; foliage becomes limp; rapid — they are not drought-tolerant.

**Overwatering:** Root rot; yellow lower leaves; stem base rot; soggy soil.

**Light stress:** Needs full sun; shade produces leggy growth with poor flowering.

**Common issues:** Aphids on new growth and flower buds; powdery mildew in damp conditions; swift decline in hot humid weather.

**Pruning:** Deadhead spent flowers regularly — this is the single most important maintenance task for continuous blooming. Cut back by a third mid-season to rejuvenate if flowering slows. Trim lightly after main flush to maintain shape.

**Seasonal notes:** Tender perennial treated as annual in cold climates. Blooms spring through autumn; may pause in peak summer heat. Replace or cut back hard in autumn.

## Gazania

**Healthy indicators:** Bright daisy-like flowers (orange/yellow/red/pink with contrasting centres); silver-grey-green foliage; low-growing rosette; drought-tolerant once established.

**Underwatering:** Flower closure (normal in low light/cool temps — not always a stress sign); wilting foliage in extreme drought.

**Overwatering:** Root rot; yellowing rosette; crown rot at soil level; poor flowering. Very susceptible — must have excellent drainage.

**Light stress:** Flowers only open in full sun — closed flowers in daytime = insufficient light, not ill health. Needs full sun for any flowering.

**Common issues:** Root/crown rot from overwatering or poor drainage; aphids; spider mites in dry hot conditions; powdery mildew.

**Pruning:** Deadhead spent flowers promptly to encourage continuous blooming — cut the stem back to the rosette. Remove dead outer leaves. Light overall trim if the plant becomes untidy. No hard pruning needed — deadheading alone is sufficient.

**Seasonal notes:** Drought-tolerant once established. Tender perennial — frost-sensitive. In containers, reduce watering significantly in winter. Flowers close at night and on cloudy days — this is normal.
